Hello, I'm Xiaoying Liao.

I am |

I am a Research Assistant in the Qiu Lab at Stanford University, working at the intersection of AI and biology. My research focuses on single-cell and spatial genomics, computational modeling of development and disease, and AI-driven biological discovery.

I have a background in both experimental and computational research. I earned my Master of Biomedical Engineering from Johns Hopkins University and my Bachelor of Science (Honours) in Economics and Statistics from the University of Sydney.

I am actively seeking Ph.D. opportunities for Fall 2027.
I am particularly interested in integrating experimental and computational approaches and using AI to discover new biological mechanisms and insights, with a special interest in AI for aging and exploring the limits of human lifespan.

Research Experience

2026–Present

Research Assistant @ Stanford University

Stanford, California, USA

Working with Prof. Xiaojie Qiu on single-cell and spatial omics to investigate cell-state transitions, tissue organization, and molecular programs underlying mammalian development and disease.

  • Human fetal heart development: Analyzing PCW7 and 8 human fetal heart spatial transcriptomics data to characterize cardiac cell states, spatial niches, cell–cell communication, and 3D reconstruction during early heart development.
  • 3D embryonic development: Building a 3D spatiotemporal atlas of mouse embryogenesis from E5.5–E7.5 to characterize cell-state transitions, spatial tissue organization, and developmental trajectories during early embryogenesis.
  • Pediatric cardiomyopathy: Leading a single-cell multi-omics and spatial transcriptomics study comparing myocardial states before and after ventricular assist device support (VAD) and between recovery and non-recovery to identify cellular programs, spatial niches, and potential biomarkers associated with myocardial recovery.
2024–2026

Research Assistant @ Johns Hopkins University

Baltimore, Maryland, USA

Worked with Prof. Carlo Iomini at the Wilmer Eye Institute to investigate cilia-dependent Hedgehog signaling in meibomian gland development and dry eye disease.

  • Meibomian gland development: Investigated how Hedgehog-responsive epithelial progenitors regulate gland development, meibocyte maturation, and lipid homeostasis.
  • Cilia–Hedgehog signaling: Characterized Ift88–Smo genetic interactions and their effects on epithelial proliferation, gland morphology, and lipid production and composition.
  • Single-cell transcriptomics: Generated and analyzed P5 and P10 meibomian gland scRNA-seq data to characterize developmental cell states and pathways associated with gland maturation and Hedgehog signaling.
2025–2026

Research Collaboration @ University of Pittsburgh

Collaborated with Dr. Huanhuan Xiao and Prof. Liyun Zhang on quantitative visual behavior analysis in zebrafish models of inherited retinal degeneration.

  • Visual behavior analysis: Analyzed photopic and scotopic visual motor response (VMR) data in pde6b mutant zebrafish to characterize genotype-dependent deficits in visual function.
  • Quantitative analysis: Developed R-based workflows for behavioral data processing, response quantification, and statistical comparison across genotypes and lighting conditions.
  • Therapeutic evaluation: Analyzed VMR responses across treatment conditions, including RA, 17-AAG, and DHA, and generated publication figures to evaluate potential rescue of visual function in the pde6b retinal degeneration model.
2023–2024

Research Assistant @ University of Sydney

Sydney, Australia

Worked with Prof. Jean Yang and Prof. Clara Grazian on computational method development and benchmarking for spatial transcriptomics.

  • Data preprocessing: Used Python and R to preprocess spatial transcriptomics datasets, including quality control, normalization, feature selection, and dimensionality reduction using principal component analysis (PCA).
  • Spatial domain detection: Applied and compared clustering approaches, including K-means and Seurat, alongside spatially informed methods such as BayesSpace and STitch3D, to identify tissue domains and characterize spatial organization.
  • Method evaluation: Benchmarked clustering performance across simulated and real datasets, examining factors affecting method efficacy and identifying strengths and limitations to inform method selection in spatial transcriptomics research.
